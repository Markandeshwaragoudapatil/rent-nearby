# Market research — India rental marketplace landscape

**Desk research date:** 27 November 2026. This is a directional scan, not a market-size study or proof of customer demand.

## 1. Competitive groups

| Platform | Public positioning observed | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| SharePal | Bengaluru-founded rental service for cameras, gaming, AV, sports and outdoor gear; its asset-partner material describes an owner/asset-partner model. | Specialist gear and owner-side partnerships are established patterns; compete on local peer supply and transparent workflows, not category novelty alone. |
| Rentomojo | Furniture/appliance subscription with KYC, refundable deposits, delivery, installation and longer tenures. | Strong operational service model, but a different job-to-be-done from one-day peer rentals. |
| Furlenco | Furniture rental, subscription and purchase options. | Convenience and lifecycle services are important for bulky goods. |
| Revv | Self-drive rental/subscription; its policy describes owner/lessor marketplace relationships for vehicles. | Handover, liability and clear rental agreements matter. Vehicle rentals are out of MVP. |
| Hygglo (Fat Llama brand merged into Hygglo in Nov 2025) | P2P rental; public help material describes lender verification, payment handling and rental protection. | Trust and protection are core marketplace infrastructure, not decorative badges. |
| Newer Indian P2P sites (RentNEarn, Rentli, ShareOnRent, etc.) | Publicly position around local listings, verified users and short-term rentals. | Direct P2P competition exists. Their public claims/metrics need independent verification before being used in investor or strategy materials. |

## 3. Observations

1. India has established managed rental operators (furniture/appliances, vehicles and specialist equipment) and a growing set of websites describing themselves as P2P rental marketplaces.
2. SharePal is particularly relevant because it is Bengaluru-founded and focuses on aspirational equipment such as camera, gaming and AV gear.
3. A broad "rent anything" proposition is not sufficient differentiation. Inventory density, product availability accuracy, response speed, trust, handover and dispute resolution will determine whether the marketplace works.
4. A local launch should concentrate on a few categories and micro-markets to solve the cold-start problem.
5. Security deposits are not platform revenue. Deposit custody, refunds, damage claims and payouts require legal, operational and payment-provider review.

## 4. Opportunities to test

- Hyperlocal search and pickup windows, with locality-level privacy before booking.
- Verified booking handover using one-time codes and timestamped condition photos.
- Accurate per-item availability and quick owner response commitments.
- Owner tools for pricing, blackout dates, booking history and earnings.
- A focused supply acquisition strategy through creator communities, colleges, event vendors and tool-owner communities.

## 5. Unknowns

- Number of active, rentable P2P items by Bengaluru micro-market.
- Renter willingness to transact with an individual rather than a rental shop.
- Owner willingness to share high-value equipment and expected protection.
- Price elasticity and acceptable deposits.
- Actual claims/dispute rate and operational cost per rental.
- Whether local pickup is acceptable or delivery is essential.

## 6. Recommended field research

Interview 15–20 likely renters and 10–15 potential owners. Recruit from creator groups, college clubs, event vendors, maker/tool communities and apartment communities. Use recent-behaviour questions rather than "would you use this?" questions. Pilot one or two dense localities before expanding Bengaluru-wide.
